# Literature inputs for the relative Section 3 proof

The fields below are mathematical arguments to the relative theorem. The table records their intended geometric meaning and the source and conventions used. The literature statements are uniform in their parameters. The arithmetic and lattice work for the specific family `P_m` is carried out in Lean.

| Interface | General result and source |
| --- | --- |
| `References.conway_fraction`, `bridge_cover` | The fraction of the rational tangle determines its two-bridge closure. Up to the choice of handedness, the oriented double branched cover is the lens space of that surgery fraction. The project defines `twoBridge p q` to select the chirality whose cover is positive `p/q` surgery. See Conway's rational tangle description and the two-bridge diagrams and branched covers in Hatcher–Thurston, *Incompressible surfaces in 2-bridge knot complements*, Invent. Math. 79 (1985), pp. 225–226. Their rational slope notation puts the odd parameter in the denominator. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01388971) |
| `SignatureReferences.evenPlumbingSignature` | The even Conway presentation bounds the linear plumbing of twisted annuli and the symmetrized Seifert matrix has the corresponding weights and unit adjacent entries. An alternating sign change on the basis puts the off-diagonals at `−1`. The sign of the matrix is tied to the orientation of the double branched cover, with the positive surgery convention used in the manuscript. See Kawauchi, *A Survey of Knot Theory*, §4.2, p. 52 and Fig. 4.2.4, and Hatcher–Thurston, p. 226. [Kawauchi](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/22445/1/Akio%20Kawauchi_.pdf) |
| `SignatureReferences.chainSignature_eq_signedPivotCount` | Right-to-left symmetric elimination has successive leading weight updates `a − 1/pivot`. Nonzero pivots allow congruence to the resulting diagonal form. Its signature is their signed count by Sylvester's law of inertia. The analogous graph elimination is stated in Saveliev, *Fukumoto–Furuta invariants of plumbed homology 3-spheres*, §2.3. [Paper](https://msp.org/pjm/2002/205-2/pjm-v205-n2-p07-p.pdf) |
| `References.minimal_crossing_changes`, `mirror_unknotting`, `signature_crossing_bound` | Unknotting number is the minimal number of changes, and mirroring a sequence gives equality of the minima. Owens gives the signed signature inequality in his introduction. The simultaneous-diagram formulation and knot-level crossing arc formulation agree, as discussed in *The Unknotting Number, Hard Unknot Diagrams, and Reinforcement Learning*, §1.1. [Owens](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0506485) · [Crossing arcs](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2025.2542174) |
| `References.owens` | Owens, *Unknotting information from Heegaard Floer homology*, Theorem 3 and Lemma 2.2(i). Theorem 3 takes `pos` positive changes and `neg = σ(K)/2` negative changes and yields a positive half-integral surgery presentation on `pos+neg` components. The integral presentation in Lemma 2.2(i) adds one 2-framed meridian to each component. The resulting meridians are mutually orthogonal, and the integral form is positive definite by the principal-minor calculation following Lemma 2.2. Here the determinant of the positive integral linking matrix is the order of the boundary's first integral homology. The handlebody has one 0-handle and no 1-handles. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2007.10.006) |
| `References.chain_plumbing`, `lens_reverse`, `lens_connected` | A chain of framed unknots realizes the corresponding negative continued fraction as a surgery coefficient. Orienting the core classes gives the adjacent signs `−1`. A lens space is a connected cyclic quotient of the sphere. Conjugating one complex coordinate replaces `q` by `−q mod p` and reverses orientation. Saveliev, §§2.2–2.3, uses negative-surgery lens notation. The archive uses positive surgery for `lens p q`, which reverses both oriented spaces in the comparison. [Linear chain surgery](https://msp.org/pjm/2002/205-2/pjm-v205-n2-p07-p.pdf) · [Lens orientation conventions](https://doi.org/10.2140/agt.2020.20.1073) |
| `References.lens_h1_Z`, `lens_h2_Z`, `lens_h1_F2`, `lens_h2_F2` | The lens-space CW complex has one cell in each dimension, `d₂=p` and `d₁=d₃=0`. It gives the integral groups `H₁=ℤ/p` and `H₂=0`, with the first and second mod-two groups computed as a quotient and kernel respectively. See Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*, Example 2.43 and §2.2. [Book](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT..pdf) |
| `References.glue`, `glued_closed`, `glued_simplyConnected`, `glued_intersection` | Collars along the chosen oriented boundary match give the smooth gluing. Van Kampen applies to the two pieces with connected overlap. Representative cycles for the piece classes can be pushed into the respective interiors, where the intersection numbers are unchanged, with cross pairings zero. See Gompf–Stipsicz, *4-Manifolds and Kirby Calculus*, and Hatcher, §1.2. [Gompf–Stipsicz](https://doi.org/10.1090/gsm/020) · [Hatcher](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT..pdf) |
| `References.mv_Z`, `mv_Z_pieces`, `mv_Z_surjective`, `mv_F2_pieces`, `mv_F2_closed` | Use the Mayer–Vietoris sequence with the collar open cover and its inclusion maps, with either integral or mod-two coefficients. The connecting map onto integral first boundary homology is surjective because the first homology of the simply connected pieces is zero. See Hatcher, §2.2. [Book](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT..pdf) |
| `References.closed_h2_torsionFree` | Poincaré duality gives `H₂(X;ℤ) ≅ H²(X;ℤ)` for the closed oriented four-manifold. Simple connectivity gives `H₁(X;ℤ)=0`. The universal coefficient theorem then identifies `H²` with a torsion-free hom group. See Hatcher, §§3.1 and 3.3. [Book](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT..pdf) |
| `References.uct_surjective`, `uct_kernel`, `coefficient_naturality` | The natural coefficient exact sequence for `0 → ℤ → ℤ → F₂ → 0` gives surjectivity of degree-two reduction when `H₁=0`. The first map is multiplication by two. Naturality gives the commutative square with the inclusion-induced maps. See Hatcher, §3.A. [Book](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT..pdf) |
| `References.donaldson` | Donaldson diagonalizes the positive definite intersection form of a closed oriented simply connected smooth four-manifold over the integers. The integral statement uses its torsion-free homology, and its dimension is left unspecified in the reference. See Donaldson, *An application of gauge theory to four-dimensional topology*, J. Differential Geom. 18 (1983), Theorem A. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214437665) |

## Section 3B references

The abbreviations and exact proof dependencies are in [SECTION3B.md](SECTION3B.md).

| Interface | General result and source |
| --- | --- |
| `FloerReferences.twoBridge_alternating` | A positive relatively prime fraction with odd numerator specifies a two-bridge knot. Its Conway normal form can have all entries of the same sign and is alternating. Reflecting the diagram preserves alternation. See Brugallé–Koseleff–Pecker, *The lexicographic degree of the first two-bridge knots*, §2. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.5802/afst.1645) |
| `OwensMinimum.attained`, `minimizing` | The minimum over characteristic covectors of one cokernel class is attained for a positive-definite form. See Owens, §1, definition of `m_Q`. Owens uses characteristic classes in the cokernel in §4. The linear lattice-translation and parity calculations for the even matrix are proved in Lean. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0506485) |
| `FloerReferences.owens_alternating` | Owens, Theorem 1, with `pos=0`, `neg=2` and `σ(K)=4`. It gives the positive surgery form with determinant `det K`, its order and parity restrictions on the entries, and one isomorphism giving both inequality and congruence against `m_G` for every class. The determinant of a knot is the order of first integral homology of its branched cover. Owens uses this equality in proving Theorem 3. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0506485) |
| `FloerReferences.goeritz_sharp` | The positive Goeritz form from an alternating diagram represents a sharp filling of its oriented double cover. Owens §4, after Figure 10, states this comparison in the orientation used here, citing Ozsváth–Szabó, *Knots with unknotting number one and Heegaard Floer homology*, Proposition 3.2. The comparison gives `m_G=d(Σ(K),·)` with the same labeling. [Owens](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0506485) · [Ozsváth–Szabó](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0401426) |
| `FloerReferences.osz_negative_lens` | Ozsváth–Szabó, *Absolutely graded Floer homologies and intersection forms for four-manifolds with boundary*, Proposition 4.8. The raw index is in `[0,p)`, which lies in the proposition's allowed range. The iteration ends with the normalized value at `S³`. The Lean proof applies the recursion to `−L(245,141)` through the oriented identification with positive `245/104` surgery. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0110170) |
| `FloerReferences.rawLabel_difference`, `rawLabel_fixed_conjugation` | In the Ozsváth–Szabó numbering, addition of a fixed generator advances raw indices by the lens denominator modulo the numerator. The inverse of that denominator therefore gives the generator with unit raw advance. In the same numbering the spin indices are the integral ones among `(p+t−1)/2` and `(t−1)/2`. Conjugation negates the centered `H²` coordinate. See Doig–Wehrli, *A combinatorial proof of the homology cobordism classification of lens spaces*, §2 and Eq. (3), and Lidman–Moore–Vazquez, §2.2 after Theorem 2.5. [Doig–Wehrli](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.06970) · [Lidman–Moore–Vazquez](https://people.vcu.edu/~moorea14/docs/bandsurgery.pdf) |
